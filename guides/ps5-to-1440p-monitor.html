Console → Monitor compatibility

PS5 to a 1440p monitor

Getting a picture is the easy part. The real compatibility question is whether the full path supports the resolution, refresh rate and gaming features you want.

Updated: 2026-09-19

Short answer: verify the monitor's HDMI input capabilities, the target refresh rate, any VRR requirement and the cable path. Do not assume a monitor's maximum DisplayPort specification also applies to its HDMI input.
Confidence: standards-backed framework • exact monitor model needs verification

What must match

1. HDMI input on the monitor

The console connects over HDMI, so the monitor's HDMI specifications matter. A monitor may advertise a high refresh rate through DisplayPort while offering different limits over HDMI.

2. Resolution and refresh rate

Check the exact combination you want, such as 1440p at a higher refresh rate. The display must accept that timing through HDMI, and the game or mode must also support the intended frame-rate target.

3. Variable refresh rate

If VRR matters to you, verify that the monitor supports a compatible VRR path over HDMI for the console. Similar marketing terms do not always mean the same implementation.

4. Cable capability

Use a cable that supports the bandwidth required by the selected video mode. If your existing cable already meets the requirement, there is no reason to replace it.

Practical checklist

  • Open the monitor’s HDMI input specs (not only the DisplayPort row).
  • Confirm 2560×1440 (or your target) at the refresh rate you want over HDMI.
  • Decide whether VRR / ALLM / HDR are required for your use case.
  • In PS5 settings, set resolution and enable 120 Hz / VRR only if the display path supports them.
  • Test with the PS5’s supplied HDMI cable first before buying another.

Common mistakes

Reading DisplayPort numbers for an HDMI console

A “240 Hz” monitor may only offer that rate over DisplayPort. The PS5 never uses DisplayPort, so HDMI limits decide what you get.

Buying HDMI 2.1 by label alone

Version numbers are a starting clue, not a proof. Always verify supported resolution, refresh and features on the exact HDMI port.

Replacing a working cable

If the supplied Ultra High Speed–class cable already delivers your target mode, keep it. Cable upgrades only help when bandwidth is the missing link.

Do you need HDMI 2.1?

That depends on the exact result you want and the monitor's implementation. Do not buy based on the version number alone; verify the supported resolution, refresh rate and features on the actual HDMI input. For cable bandwidth, Ultra High Speed HDMI and when to keep the supplied cable, see the PS5 HDMI 2.1 cable path guide. For 4K/120 on a specific Dell model, see the PS5 → Dell U2725QE compatibility page.

When to keep what you have

Keep your current monitor and cable when HDMI already supports your target 1440p refresh and any VRR you care about. Upgrade only the missing link—monitor HDMI capability, console settings, or cable bandwidth—not the whole stack by default.

FAQ

Will any 1440p monitor work with a PS5?

Most will show a picture over HDMI, but high refresh rates and VRR depend on the monitor’s HDMI input capabilities—not only DisplayPort marketing specs.

Do I need HDMI 2.1 for 1440p on PS5?

It depends on the resolution, refresh rate and features you want. Verify supported timings on the actual HDMI input rather than buying on the version label alone.

Should I replace the cable that came with the PS5?

Usually no. Keep the supplied cable if it already supports your target mode.

Why does DisplayPort refresh look higher than HDMI on my monitor?

Many monitors advertise peak refresh over DisplayPort while offering lower limits on HDMI. The PS5 uses HDMI only.
