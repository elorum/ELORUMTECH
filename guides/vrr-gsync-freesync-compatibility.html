VRR, G-Sync and FreeSync: what works with what

VRR goes by several names: HDMI VRR, VESA Adaptive-Sync, AMD FreeSync and NVIDIA G-SYNC Compatible. Whether it works depends on the source, the display and every cable, dock or adapter between them. Here is what each standards body and maker documents, with every claim sourced.

Updated: 2026-10-23

By Nicki A.

Last reviewed: 2026-10-23 by ELORUMTECH against the primary sources listed below · How we check compatibility

Short answer: Console → TV: HDMI 2.1 port and a VRR-capable TV; PS5 uses HDMI 2.1 VRR [PS2], and Xbox Series X|S list both HDMI VRR and AMD FreeSync [XB1][XB2]. PC → monitor: a direct DisplayPort cable carries Adaptive-Sync on GeForce GTX 10 series and higher [NV3] and on AMD FreeSync [AMD1], and Intel Arc uses VESA Adaptive Sync, also over an HDMI 2.1 input [IN1]; otherwise over HDMI you need HDMI 2.1 VRR [HF1], or FreeSync over HDMI on AMD [AMD1]. Mac: for Adaptive Sync use a Thunderbolt or USB-C cable, not an HDMI adapter [AP1]; the Mac models Apple lists also get VRR from their own HDMI port with an Ultra High Speed 48 Gbps HDMI cable, no adapter [AP2]. Docks, adapters and MST hubs are not covered by these sources in general; AMD lists FreeSync compatible docks for supported Ryzen AI 400 Series devices [AMD1].
Confidence: maker-documented • docks and adapters need the maker’s confirmation

What VRR does

VRR lets a console or computer deliver frames as fast as it can, which is often slower than the display’s fixed refresh rate [HL1]. By waiting until the next frame is ready before sending it, VRR gives smoother gameplay, and HDMI Licensing Administrator says it reduces or eliminates lag, judder and frame tearing [HL1]. HDMI Forum lists VRR among the HDMI 2.1 gaming features [HF1], next to ALLM and QFT [HL2].

HDMI VRR, Adaptive-Sync, FreeSync and G-SYNC Compatible

  • HDMI VRR is part of HDMI 2.1 [HF1]. Xbox Series X and PS5 were the first consoles to support the latest HDMI gaming features [HL2].
  • VESA Adaptive-Sync is part of the DisplayPort standard [V2] and was first supported in DisplayPort 1.2a [V1]. To earn VESA’s AdaptiveSync certification, PC and laptop monitors must pass more than 50 test criteria [V2].
  • AMD FreeSync uses DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync and HDMI VRR [AMD1], plus FreeSync over HDMI on displays without HDMI 2.1 [AMD1]. It has three tiers: FreeSync, FreeSync Premium and FreeSync Premium Pro [AMD1]. AMD notes that VESA AdaptiveSync certification does not necessarily mean FreeSync certification [AMD1].
  • NVIDIA G-SYNC Compatible is a display NVIDIA has validated for a basic VRR experience without an NVIDIA processor in the monitor [NV1]. Displays not on the list can still be tried from the NVIDIA Control Panel [NV5].

VRR not working? Common fixes

  1. Turn VRR on in the display itself. AMD asks you to enable FreeSync in the display’s settings [AMD1]; NVIDIA notes some Adaptive Sync monitors ship with VRR disabled [NV4]; some TVs call it “game mode” [AP2].
  2. Use the TV’s HDMI 2.1 port. Sony asks whether the PS5 is on an HDMI 2.1 port [PS1], and Microsoft notes that on some TVs only specific HDMI ports support HDMI 2.1 [XB3].
  3. Update the TV or monitor firmware. Many TV makers added these features through firmware updates [XB3].
  4. Turn it on at the source. PS5: Screen and Video settings [PS1]. Xbox: the VRR menu with Always On, Gaming Only or Off [XB4]. NVIDIA: the NVIDIA Control Panel [NV5]. Mac: Variable or Adaptive refresh rate [AP1].
  5. Remove adapters, docks and hubs while testing. For Adaptive Sync, Apple says not to use an HDMI cable or adapter [AP1]; on the Mac models Apple lists for HDMI VRR, connect the HDMI cable directly from the Mac’s HDMI port with no adapter [AP2]. NVIDIA lists a native DisplayPort connection [NV4]. On NVIDIA, set the monitor to DisplayPort 1.2a or higher in its menu [NV4].
  6. Check the output mode. On PS5, VRR is disabled during 8K output [PS1].
  7. Tearing above the refresh rate? If your frame rate regularly exceeds the display’s refresh rate, AMD suggests V-Sync or a frame-rate cap [AMD1].
  8. Flicker? Adaptive Sync may flicker outside games or on displays that are not validated [AP3]; turn variable refresh rate off [AP1], or set VRR to Gaming Only or Off on Xbox [XB4].

Before you buy anything

  1. Check the display’s spec page for HDMI 2.1 VRR, Adaptive-Sync, FreeSync or G-SYNC Compatible, per input.
  2. Connect the VRR display directly to the console or graphics card and confirm VRR works there.
  3. Only then add a dock or adapter the maker lists for VRR.
  4. Still stuck? See monitor stuck at 60 Hz or external monitor not detected.

FAQ

What is VRR?

VRR (variable refresh rate) lets a console or computer send each frame as soon as it is ready, and the display refreshes to match instead of at a fixed rate. HDMI Licensing Administrator says this reduces or eliminates lag, judder and frame tearing. On HDMI it is the HDMI 2.1 VRR feature; on DisplayPort it is VESA Adaptive-Sync, which AMD FreeSync and NVIDIA G-SYNC Compatible build on.

Is G-SYNC Compatible the same as FreeSync?

Both run on standard VRR, but they are different labels. G-SYNC Compatible means NVIDIA has validated a monitor for a basic VRR experience on GeForce cards. FreeSync is AMD’s certification with three tiers: FreeSync, FreeSync Premium and FreeSync Premium Pro. AMD says GeForce 10 series and newer cards that support DisplayPort Adaptive-Sync are expected to work with FreeSync displays, while NVIDIA warns that a display it has not validated may work, work partly or not work at all.

Does the PS5 support VRR?

Yes, over HDMI 2.1. Sony says VRR is enabled automatically for supported games when the PS5 is connected to an HDMI 2.1 VRR-compatible TV or PC monitor, and you can also apply it to unsupported games. Use the TV’s HDMI 2.1 port. VRR is disabled during 8K output.

Does the Xbox Series X|S support FreeSync?

Yes. Microsoft’s spec pages for both Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S list HDMI Variable Refresh Rate and AMD FreeSync, over a single HDMI 2.1 port. In the console’s video settings you can set VRR to Always On, Gaming Only or Off.

Does VRR work through a dock, adapter or MST hub?

Only if the maker says so. None of the standards bodies or GPU makers we checked promise VRR through docks in general, through a DisplayPort-to-HDMI adapter or through an MST hub. The one exception we found is maker-specific: AMD keeps a list of FreeSync compatible docks for supported Ryzen AI 400 Series devices. Apple says to use a Thunderbolt, USB-C or DisplayPort cable for Adaptive Sync and not an HDMI cable or adapter; on the Mac models Apple lists for HDMI VRR, connect the HDMI cable directly from the Mac’s HDMI port, and to get the highest resolution and refresh rate don’t use an adapter with it. NVIDIA’s requirements list a G-SYNC Compatible monitor connected natively via DisplayPort. For any other dock, adapter or hub, check its maker’s documentation.

Can I use VRR over HDMI with an NVIDIA graphics card?

Yes, with an HDMI 2.1 display, but NVIDIA’s two pages differ on which cards. The footnote on NVIDIA’s G-SYNC monitor list says GeForce 30 series and higher on HDMI 2.1. An older NVIDIA support article, last updated in 2021, says GeForce GTX 16 series/RTX 20 series and higher on HDMI 2.1 displays. Over DisplayPort, both say GeForce 10 series and higher.

Why does my screen flicker with VRR on?

Apple says that if you are not gaming, or the display is not validated, Adaptive Sync may cause the screen to flicker. Turn variable refresh rate off for desktop work, or use a setting such as Gaming Only on Xbox. Apple also says to make sure the display is using the latest firmware from the manufacturer.
