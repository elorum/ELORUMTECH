No signal, wrong resolution or stuck at 60 Hz? Find the limiting link

Find out why your monitor shows no signal or the wrong refresh rate, and whether your USB-C, DisplayPort, HDMI or dock connection can carry the mode you want — part by part, with sources.

Updated: 2026-10-23

By Nicki A. · Last reviewed: 2026-10-23 against the sources listed below

One tool, three problems: no picture on an external monitor, a monitor stuck at a lower refresh rate than it is sold with, and USB-C, DisplayPort, HDMI or dock limits. Describe what you have; the solver checks each part of the connection, tells you which part decides the result, and gives fix steps from Microsoft and dock-maker documentation.

How the solver decides

It splits the connection into parts — computer output, anything in between, cable and the monitor input — and checks each one. A connection is only as good as its weakest part.

  • FAIL means a documented limit rules your mode out. Example: 3440×1440 at 165 Hz needs at least 19.62 Gbit/s of active pixels at 8-bit RGB, more than an HDMI 2.0-class link’s 18 Gbit/s [H1].
  • UNKNOWN means nothing rules it out, but something needed is missing, often the monitor’s exact timing for that input or a cable certification.
  • CONDITIONAL means the numbers fit with a full timing you supplied, and the listed checks still have to hold.
  • PASS needs the exact monitor model to list your mode for that input, plus documented capability on every part. A quick check from spec-sheet classes never says PASS.

The numbers are separated on purpose. Raw link rate (32.4 Gbit/s for DisplayPort HBR3 over four lanes) is not the same as usable payload (25.92 Gbit/s) [V1] [V2]. And a cable label like “8K 60Hz” describes one mode on one standard; it is not a 60 Hz ceiling for your monitor.

Which links can carry common modes

Active pixels × refresh × bits per pixel gives the minimum bitrate. Real signals add blanking, so a “Not ruled out” cell can still fail with the monitor’s real timing. DisplayPort payloads are VESA figures [V1] [V3]; HDMI payloads are DERIVED from the published raw totals [H1] [H2].

FAQ

Why does the solver say FAIL for my refresh rate?

FAIL means a documented link or part limit already rules the mode out. Example on this page: 3440×1440 at 165 Hz needs more active-pixel bandwidth than an HDMI 2.0-class 18 Gbit/s link can carry.

What does UNKNOWN mean in the result?

UNKNOWN means nothing on the page rules the mode out, but something required is still missing — often the monitor’s exact timing for that input, or a cable certification.

When can the solver say PASS?

PASS needs the exact monitor model to list your mode for that input, plus documented capability on every part of the chain. A quick check from spec-sheet classes never says PASS.

Why check each part of the cable/dock chain?

The connection is only as good as its weakest part: computer output, anything in between (dock or adapter), the cable, and the monitor input. The solver splits those parts and checks each one.

Where do the bandwidth numbers come from?

The page separates calculated active-pixel bandwidth from full-timing payloads and cites the VESA, HDMI and other sources listed in the Sources section. Where a figure is missing, the result stays UNKNOWN instead of inventing one.
