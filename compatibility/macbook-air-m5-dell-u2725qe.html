MacBook Air M5 → Dell U2725QE

Updated: 2026-10-18

Verdict · Manufacturer-confirmed

Compatible

Short answer: yes, per Apple and Dell specs. Thunderbolt 4 (USB-C) into the U2725QE’s Thunderbolt 4 upstream port carries 4K at up to 120 Hz (the monitor’s maximum), plus USB and up to 140 W charging from the monitor. Apple lists up to two external displays for the 13-inch Air M5; a single display can run up to 4K at 240 Hz (Apple tech specs 126320; also Apple MacBook Air specs). Dell specifies 3840×2160 up to 120 Hz with Thunderbolt 4 upstream and up to 140 W Power Delivery (Dell U2725QE tech specs). Dell lists a Thunderbolt 4 downstream (daisy-chain) port on the U2725QE and Apple supports two external displays on the MacBook Air M5, but whether that exact daisy-chain pairing works is not confirmed. What you need

Use a Thunderbolt 4 cable capable of carrying display data and power. For this pairing, a separate video adapter is not inherently required.

Target: 3840×2160 up to 120 Hz (Dell panel limit; Apple source supports higher refresh on a single display).
Charging: Dell documents up to 140 W PD on Thunderbolt upstream—more than the Air typically needs; PD negotiates the level. Apple does not state that charging and display must run on the same port at once; the one-cable path relies on Dell’s TB4 upstream with PD.
One cable: Apple + Dell specs support video, USB data and charging on this Thunderbolt path when a compliant TB4 cable is used.

Why ELORUMTECH says yes

Apple’s MacBook Air (13-inch, M5) tech specs (126320) document two Thunderbolt 4 USB-C ports with Charging, DisplayPort, TB4/USB4 40 Gb/s, DisplayPort 1.4/HBR3 with DSC, and up to two external displays (two at 6K60 or 4K144; one at 8K60, 5K120 or 4K240), including two displays over a single Thunderbolt port. The 15-inch M5 Air is a separate spec page and is not covered here. Dell documents Thunderbolt 4 upstream with DisplayPort 1.4/DSC, 140 W EPR Power Delivery, and a maximum 4K/120 Hz panel mode on the U2725QE. The source and destination therefore share a direct Thunderbolt/DisplayPort path. Dell lists a Thunderbolt 4 downstream (daisy-chain) port on the U2725QE and Apple supports two external displays on the MacBook Air M5, but whether that exact daisy-chain pairing works is not confirmed.

Practical checklist for this pair

  • Use a Thunderbolt 4–rated cable (not a charge-only USB-C cable).
  • Connect to the monitor’s Thunderbolt 4 upstream port, not a downstream-only USB-C port.
  • In macOS Displays settings, confirm 3840×2160 and the desired refresh rate.
  • Confirm charging status in macOS if one-cable power is part of your goal.
  • If the cable already meets Thunderbolt 4 requirements, keep it.

Common mistakes

  • Using a passive charge-only USB-C cable that cannot carry high-bandwidth video.
  • Assuming any USB-C port on the monitor is Thunderbolt upstream.
  • Buying an HDMI or DisplayPort adapter when the direct Thunderbolt path already works.
  • Adding a dock solely to connect these two devices when the monitor already provides upstream Thunderbolt, PD and USB expansion.

Keep yours

If you already have a compliant Thunderbolt 4 cable, ELORUMTECH does not identify a reason to buy an adapter or dock just to connect these two devices.

When a dock still helps

A Thunderbolt dock becomes useful only if you need more ports than the U2725QE provides, or a different desk layout. For one-cable 4K/120 multi-port setups in general, see the Thunderbolt dock guide. For generic USB-C monitor paths, see the USB-C laptop → 4K monitor guide.

Confidence: Manufacturer-confirmed. Exact refresh-rate availability can still depend on macOS settings, cable quality and the rest of the active display configuration.

FAQ

Does MacBook Air M5 need an adapter for the Dell U2725QE?

No adapter is inherently required. Use Thunderbolt 4 (USB-C) into the U2725QE Thunderbolt 4 upstream input.

Can this pair do 4K at 120 Hz on one cable?

Yes, with a compliant Thunderbolt 4 cable carrying video, USB data and charging on the documented path.

Will the Dell U2725QE charge a MacBook Air M5?

Dell specifies up to 140 W PD on Thunderbolt upstream—more than the Air typically needs; PD negotiates the level.

Should I buy a dock for this pairing?

Not just to connect these two devices. Keep a compliant Thunderbolt 4 cable; add a dock only for extra ports beyond the monitor.
